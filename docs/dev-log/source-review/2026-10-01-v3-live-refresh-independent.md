## Automatic FA-rank design-plan judgment

**PASS as a proposed, design-only coordination plan; criterion and implementation contract remain unfrozen.** `docs/design/auto-fa-rank-design-plan-20261001.md:12-16,28-37` explicitly makes the criterion, rank-zero representation, eligibility/failure policy and boundary/payload wording research decisions, with no fit or code authorized. The plan rightly avoids claiming a meaningful selector over the current singleton `{1}` and asks whether a diagonal genetic covariance (`K=0` with positive `psi`) can be compared with one shared factor (`K=1`): lines 20-26. Under full-column-rank factors, its rotational-degree count `q_G(K)=TK-K(K-1)/2+T` gives 4, 8 and 11 for T=4 and K=0,1,2, respectively; K=2 exceeds the ten entries in an unconstrained symmetric G. The plan correctly states this count is necessary but not sufficient for identifiability (`:43-49`). Its `vec(U)` covariance is dimensionally consistent when U is pedigree-members × traits and `A` is the pedigree relationship matrix. The named validation cases, full attempt-table retention, fixed-rank escape and explicit exclusion of Poisson GLLVM selection are appropriate at `:72-78,39-41`.

Before freezing a future criterion, add two precise items to ticket C0 / the validation plan; these are **future design decisions, not V3 blockers**:

- State that K=0 is nested in K=1 at zero factor loading, a singular/boundary point. A regular chi-square likelihood-ratio reference and a routine BIC parameter penalty cannot be assumed from the 4-versus-8 count alone. Justify the chosen same-data ML/REML comparison and penalty for that nonregular case, or label it a pragmatic selection rule with its validation limits. Current plan `:33,45-49,74-78` leaves this unfrozen, so no incorrect selector is asserted yet.
- Require an explicit identifiability witness for separating genetic `A`-structured covariance from unstructured residual `I` covariance under the actual pedigree/record design. Parameter count alone cannot separate G from R when the covariance kernels give little or no independent information. Plan `:49` already warns of this; make it a concrete acceptance case, including a refusal or uncertainty report for an uninformative pedigree.
